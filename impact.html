Learning impact & classroom use

More than a game. A tool for teaching.

From daily review to classroom-based research, Classroom Quest can support different learning activities. The value comes from how teachers design and facilitate the experience.

i
About educational impact: The outcomes below are potential benefits, not proven improvements in scores or learning. Classroom Quest is in classroom testing; any research or impact claims should be supported by actual data and an appropriate study design.
For learners

Participation with a purpose.

A friendly team challenge can bring energy to review activities and encourage learners to retrieve, discuss, and apply what they know.

Teachers can shape the experience through question difficulty, team roles, supportive feedback, and fair opportunities to participate.

Active recall and practiceReview previously taught ideas through short, question-based rounds.
Motivation and participationTeam competition may encourage involvement when paired with inclusive classroom routines.
Immediate formative signalsNotice topics that need reteaching from the types of questions learners miss.
Communication and teamworkUse team roles and post-question discussions to involve more learners, not just the phone holders.

Practical lessons from testing

What the first classroom dry run taught us.

Early classroom observations are useful for improving the app, but they are not a formal effectiveness study.

What looked promising

Participating learners showed enthusiasm and a desire to win. Some questions were deliberately challenging to encourage deeper thinking.

What still needs work

Classmates who were not playing sometimes became distracted. The current game also uses multiple-choice questions only. Future activities should provide meaningful roles for non-playing learners.

Teaching & research guidance

Use it responsibly and effectively.

Short guidance for teachers planning classroom activities, intervention projects, or research.

How can I involve learners who are not holding the phones?
Rotate team members each round; assign observers, explainers, score analysts, and peer-support roles. Invite the class to discuss why an answer is correct after the question closes.
Can I use this for an action research study?
Yes, as an intervention or learning activity worth investigating—but not as proof of effectiveness by itself. Define measurable outcomes, use appropriate baseline and follow-up measures, record implementation fidelity, and follow your school or institution's research and consent requirements.
Can it replace a formal assessment?
It is best viewed as a supplementary formative and practice tool. The current multiple-choice format and team-based nature may not represent every learner's independent mastery. Follow school assessment policies for formal grading.
What about learner privacy and access?
Avoid recording full learner names or sensitive information in team names or question content. Use a password-protected, trusted local network; keep the classroom laptop secure and do not expose the local server to the public internet.
